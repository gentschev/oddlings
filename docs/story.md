# Oddlings: story and world

Working title: **Oddlings**. Everything here is original. Do not borrow names, characters or
looks from existing games or shows.

## Premise

In a valley called **Tally Vale** live small creatures called **Oddlings**. Every Oddling
wears a number pattern on its body: dots, tiles, stripes, segments. The valley runs on
counting. The baker counts buns, the bridge keeper counts planks, the night heron counts
stars.

Lately the counts come out wrong. One bun short. One plank short. One star short. Where
something went missing there is a mark on the ground: a ring. When a count goes wrong near
an Oddling, the Oddling gets **muddled**: its pattern scrambles and it turns grumpy and
confused. The valley says a thief called **the Taker** is doing it.

The player is a child who has just arrived in the valley and turns out to be a **Counter**:
someone who can set a muddled count right. With a partner Oddling, the player travels the
valley, un-muddles Oddlings (who then become friends and join the team), and follows the
ring marks to find out what is really happening.

The chapter order follows the order the facts are taught in `docs/practice-engine.md`.

## The truth (revealed across chapters 2 to 4)

There is no thief. The ring marks belong to **Nought**, a shy ring-shaped Oddling. Nought's
pattern is nothing at all: an empty ring. Long ago the other Oddlings told Nought "You are
nothing. You do not count." Nought went away.

When Nought left, the valley lost its way to write any number past nine. Counts could only
go up to 9, so anything past the ninth thing fell out of the count and faded. Those are the
missing buns, planks and stars. Nought has been following the fading things around, trying
to hold on to them, which is why the rings are always there.

The ending of the first arc: the valley needs to build the **Ten Bridge** and cannot,
because nobody can make ten. The player brings Nought back. One and Nought stand together:
**10**. Nought does not add anything (5 + 0 = 5), and that is exactly why Nought can hold a
place for everyone else. The faded things come back.

What the story is saying, in the child's terms:
- Zero is not "nothing that doesn't matter". It holds a place. Without it there is no ten.
- Somebody who feels like nothing may be the one everybody else needs.
- When a thing goes wrong, ask what happened before you blame someone.

The maths in the story is true and is the maths the child is practising: adding zero,
making ten, teen numbers as ten and some more.

## Tone

Warm, a little strange, funny in small ways. Odd side characters with one clear trait each
(a snail who sells hats but has no head for them; a frog who counts everything twice to be
safe). A real mystery with clues the child can notice before the characters do: the ring
marks appear in the background of scenes from the first minute. No villains, no fighting
to hurt, no peril beyond "oh no, the count is wrong".

## Encounters are "muddles", not battles

A muddled Oddling blocks the path. Its pattern is scrambled and it has a wobbly grey cloud
(the **muddle**) around it. The player's Oddling helps sort it out. Each correct answer
makes the partner do a helping move and shrinks the muddle. When the muddle is gone the
wild Oddling's pattern snaps back into place, it cheers, and it may ask to join.

There is no losing. A wrong answer makes the muddle puff up a little and the partner looks
puzzled; then the game shows how the fact works and asks again shortly after.

## Cast for the first arc

| Name | What it is | Number idea it carries | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tenny** | turtle whose shell is a 2-by-5 grid of ten tiles that light up | ten frame, making ten | starter choice |
| **Domi** | block-shaped puppy whose body is a domino | doubles, parts of a number | starter choice |
| **Inch** | caterpillar whose segments are a number line | counting on and back | starter choice |
| **Moss** | very old, slow snail-like Oddling who keeps the Count Book | none shown (shell is a spiral) | mentor; gives quests; reads the book |
| **Nought** | shy floating ring | zero | the mystery; joins at the end of arc 1 |
| **Fivel** | starfish-like, five arms | five, five-and-some | first friend made in chapter 1 |
| **Twoot** | small owl, everything in twos | plus two, even numbers | chapter 1 or 2 |
| **Bix** | beetle with dice-dot wing cases | subitising dots | chapter 1 |
| **Teeny** | meerkat-like, always stands on a little stack of ten | teen numbers (ten and some) | chapter 4 |

Each Oddling's body is a working maths picture. When a question is asked, the partner's
pattern can light up to show it (Tenny's shell shows 7 lit and 3 dark for 7 + 3).

Oddlings **grow** in three forms (small, middle, big) as the child masters the facts that
Oddling carries. Growth is driven by the learner model, never by purchases or timers.

## Chapters of the first arc

Each chapter is one area of the map and one band of skills. Story beats are short: two to
five lines of dialogue each, with the voice reading every line aloud.
Every story beat must show the thing it talks about, so the child can point to it.

1. **Pebble Path** (facts within 5, plus and minus 0 and 1, seeing small dot groups).
   Arrive, meet Moss and choose a partner. Bix hosts dots, Tenny ten frames, and Inch
   plus zero and plus one. Find the ring after plus one. Inch then hosts minus one,
   Twoot plus two, Fivel adding and taking away within five, and Twoot minus two. Each
   pal joins at its first completed skill stop. Domi comes in area 2 unless chosen first.
   Things are going missing one at a time. Moss points to the ring where a bun was.
2. **Twin Falls** (doubles, near doubles, halves). Twoot guides the child. Arrive at
   the falls, help Domi with doubles, and find a den behind the falls. Its walls have
   ring marks and a torn page from the Count Book. Help Twoot with near doubles, then
   Domi with halves. A round shape watches from the mist and slips away. The page shows
   that the old counts went higher than nine. Why did they stop?
3. **The Tenwood** (pairs that make 10, facts within 10). Trees here grow ten nuts each
   and every tree is short. A shape hides in a hollow tree. It is Nought. Nought runs.
   A muddled Oddling says "It is the Taker!" Moss is not so sure.
4. **Teen Hills and the Ten Bridge** (teen numbers as ten and more, adding across ten).
   The bridge needs ten planks and the valley can only count nine. The player finds
   Nought, hears what happened, and brings Nought back. 1 and 0 make 10.

Later arcs (not for the first builds): bigger numbers with Nought on the team, taking
away across ten, tens and ones, and a second mystery.

## Writing rules for all in-game text

- First grade reading level. Short sentences, at most 8 words. One idea per line.
- Mostly short, common, decodable words. New or longer words (muddle, Oddling, Nought)
  are introduced one at a time, said aloud, and reused.
- Every line of text has matching recorded audio and a "say it again" button.
- Never more than two lines on screen at once.
- Praise effort and thinking ("You worked it out!"), not being smart.
- No text is required to play a muddle: the question is numbers and pictures.

## Sample lines (chapter 1 opening)

- Moss: "Oh! A new face. Hello."
- Moss: "I am Moss. I keep the Count Book."
- Moss: "We had six buns. Now we have five."
- Moss: "The count is wrong. It makes us muddled."
- Moss: "Pick a pal. Go and help."
- (after the first muddle) Fivel: "My dots are back! Thank you!"
- (first ring mark) Moss: "Stop! Look at the ground."
- Moss: "A ring mark. Right here."
- Moss: "A bun sat here. Now it is gone."
- Moss: "Who made the ring? Odd. Very odd."

## Pebble Path hosts

The nine skill stops follow teaching order: Bix (`see-dots`), Tenny (`see-frame`),
Inch (`add-plus0`, `add-plus1`, `sub-minus01`), Twoot (`add-plus2`), Fivel (`add-within5`,
`sub-within5`), then Twoot (`sub-minus2-all`). Welcome is the first stone and the ring
story is between plus one and minus one. Camp is the house, not a stone. Later-area sets
stay with their existing pals. The Oddbook's area 1 ownership comes from these hosts.

## Twin Falls hosts and beats

The six stones are falls (Twoot speaks), doubles (Domi), den (Twoot speaks), near
doubles (Twoot), halves (Domi), and mist (Twoot speaks). Domi joins at the doubles stop
if not chosen as the starter. Twoot carries near doubles in the Oddbook as well as twos.
The return arch leads to Pebble Path; the tent opens camp.

Falls, with both falls, trees and rocks visible:
1. "Hoo! You made it to Twin Falls."
2. "We have two of everything here."
3. "Two falls. Two trees. Two big rocks."
4. "But our counts come out wrong too."
5. "Help my pals. Then look for clues."

Den, with wall marks and the whole page clear of the dialogue:
1. "Look! A den behind the falls."
2. "Ring marks. All over the walls."
3. "And a page from the Count Book!"
4. "It counts past nine. Ten. Eleven. Twelve."
5. "Why did the counts stop at nine?"

Wall marks sparkle on line two. The page is outlined and pulses on line three.

Mist, with a faint round shape in front of the falls:
1. "Hush. Look at the falls."
2. "A round shape, in the mist."
3. "It looks at us. It looks sad."
4. "Wait! Do not go!"
5. "It is gone. We must find it."

Nought appears on line two, leaves on line four, and is gone by line five. Every story
beat must show the thing it talks about; dialogue alone is not a clue.
